Pygin NNUE Inspector

Loads a Pygin .nnue v1 net and evaluates positions in the browser with the same integer arithmetic the engine uses. Shows what each piece is worth to the net, and how the 16 threat scalars — which HalfKP-style nets don't have — contribute. Your net is read locally and never uploaded.

Expecting a KA8T net: 6144 inputs, 8 king buckets, 16 threats, QA 127 / QB 64. The layer widths are read from the file, so every shipped net loads whatever its tail is (v1 and v2 are 32 wide, v3 onward 16). A net whose INPUT layout or quantisation differs is rejected rather than silently mis-read.

What the reference check proves

40 positions were exported from the C engine with their feature indices, their 16 threat bytes, and their exact evaluation. Feature and threat agreement is net-independent, so it is verified for any net you load — that is the part most likely to drift, because it re-implements bitboard attack generation. The evaluation column is only checked when the loaded net is the one the vectors were generated from (8cc4d9d6caeb); with any other net it is reported as skipped rather than quietly passed.

Threat vector (T16)

TermWhiteBlack Balance

These 16 bytes are fed to the tail alongside the two 256-wide accumulator halves, ordered side-to-move first. Each is a population count scaled by a fixed factor and clamped at 127 — a clamped value has saturated and cannot rise further, which is worth knowing when a term looks unresponsive.

Feature transformer

KA8T orients the board to each side, mirrors it when the king sits on the e–h files, and picks one of 8 king buckets. Both perspectives index the same 6144×256 table; the tail sees them concatenated, side to move first.
